One turn at a time per Conversation

Every Trigger goes through the Router into a queue. The Bot works on one batch at a time, and you can skip the line.

About 14 minutes · a browser · you'll write runAfter(receivedAts), the rule that decides when a batch of quick Messages starts its Turn

Words you'll need

Trigger
One event that wakes a Bot. A Message you send is one.
Router
What every Trigger passes through. It drops repeats and queues the rest.
Conversation queue
The Triggers of one Conversation that no Turn has taken yet.

Warm-up · Lesson 11

The process running a Turn dies. What brings the Turn back?

  1. The user sends the Message again
  2. The reaper re-queues the job when its lease expires, and a process replays it.
  3. The web server starts the Turn over

1The Router drops repeats

Slide 19 says the server "drops any ID it has already seen". Here the unique ID is the user Message's own id. The web client makes a uuidv7 for each send and passes it as id to bot.send. If the request fails on the network, the client tries again with the same id, up to 3 tries in all.

sendToBot looks for the id first, under the Conversation's row lock and before the Quota check. A repeat saves nothing, so a retry works while its own Turn runs and after the Quota is spent. Its stream joins the Turn that took the Message. If that Turn has ended, the stream gives the Turn's Messages and then turnEnded. An id that belongs to another Conversation is refused with BAD_REQUEST, and the answer says nothing about that row.

2One Turn at a time, by chaining

Each Trigger is a row in chat.bot_trigger. It keeps the source's unique ID, its Conversation, the Message it carries, received_at and turn_id. Its source (user) and channel (web) say where it came from, so a Turn's provenance is the Triggers it took. The Conversation queue is the rows with no turn_id.

receive saves the Trigger. If no Turn is running, it starts one that takes everything waiting. If one is running, the Trigger waits. When a Turn ends, turnEnded starts the next Turn for all the Triggers that arrived meanwhile. No job stays alive between Turns, and each Turn is still one runner job.

Every transaction that starts or ends a Turn takes the Conversation's row lock first. A partial unique index on running Turns (bot_turn_running_bot_id_idx) turns a missed lock into an error, never a second running Turn.

3Quick Messages make one Turn

A new Turn does not start at once. Its job runs when 500 ms pass with no new Trigger, and never later than 2.5 s after the first Trigger it took. There is no cap. This is your exercise.

While the job is queued and has recorded no Checkpoint, a new Trigger joins that Turn. The runner has no reschedule call, so the Router cancels the queued job and queues a new one with a later runAfter, in the same transaction. The job's first Checkpoint, start_turn, sets the Turn's startedAt. The Turn's input is the user Messages saved before that, so it holds the whole batch.

4Skipping the line: interrupt or Stop

In your 1:1 Conversation, a Message sent after the Turn's job has started does not wait. It interrupts. sendToBot saves the Message, queues its Trigger and cancels the running job. The Turn aborts at its next Checkpoint or heartbeat (up to 10 s), ends stopped and calls turnEnded. What it already sent stays. The next Turn takes the new Message, and the first Turn's transcript is part of its input. Several sends during the abort all join that one Turn. CONFLICT and its toast are gone.

Stop (stopBot) is different. In the same transaction it marks every waiting Trigger as taken by the Turn it stops, so the queue is empty and nothing follows. Those Messages stay in the chat, and the next Turn reads them as history.

A cancelled job whose process dies never sees the cancel. The reaper's sweep finds it and calls the job's onCancelled hook, which ends the Turn stopped and chains the next one, so a Conversation is never stuck on "Working…".

Try it

Quiz

A Turn is running and two of your Messages wait in the queue. You press Stop. What happens?

  1. A new Turn starts and answers both Messages
  2. Both Messages are deleted
  3. The Turn ends stopped and nothing follows. The next Turn reads both Messages as history.

Key takeaways

If you remember only one thing: every Trigger goes through the Router into the Conversation queue, and the next Turn takes everything that arrived while the last one ran.

  • A repeat is the same Message. The client's id is the Message's id, so a resend saves nothing and joins the same Turn.
  • Quick Messages batch. The job runs 500 ms after the newest Trigger, and no later than 2.5 s after the first.
  • You skip the line. A new Message interrupts the running Turn. Stop ends it and starts nothing.

Go deeper: sources, side notes and a challenge

The lecture

  • Slide 14, "What wakes the agent": "Router: drops duplicates, picks the conversation", then "Conversation queue: one turn at a time". Its footer says every wake-up records where it came from, so the agent can tell whether you asked, a routine ran or an outside service sent it.
  • Slide 19, "What happens when you hit send": the app "saves the message locally with a unique ID" and "the server drops any ID it has already seen". Caption: "Unique IDs make retries safe", and for the turn, "One turn at a time per conversation."
  • Slide 20, "Rules for handling new messages": you, in a 1:1 chat, "skip the line", and a new message or the Stop button "interrupts or stops the turn". "3 quick messages → 1 turn". "The agent works on one batch of messages at a time." Everyone else joins a FIFO line. Step 3 builds the line for the owner only, because no other wake source exists yet.

Side notes

  • The batching numbers are a recorded deviation. The lecture gives none, so they come from the research ticket Prior art: per-conversation queues, skipping the line and batching, and the decision is in Batching quick Messages into one Turn. 500 ms is OpenClaw's queue debounce default, and 2.5 s is its rule that a batch closes at 5 times the debounce. Hermes's 0.35 s and 1.0 s were rejected as tuned for a fast phone typist, too tight for three deliberate Messages. The constants are batchQuietMs and batchLongestMs in router.ts.
  • There is no cap on a batch because only the owner can wake a Bot in step 3, and the 2.5 s limit already bounds it. A later source that can flood the line picks a cap in its own step.
  • Steering (sending a Message into the running Turn) was rejected: the runner cannot send into a running job, and splicing a Message into a replayed workflow needs a Checkpoint-safe design (Skipping the line: interrupt vs Stop).
  • The runner stays generic. It has no partitions or priority; the queue rule lives in the Bot. Its only addition is the onCancelled hook on a job definition.
  • Provenance is stored but not shown to the Model: every Trigger is the user in step 3 (Trigger provenance: recording where a wake-up came from). The first step that adds a second source chooses the format.
  • Tests: test/core/server/routers/bot-send.integration.test.ts (the "one Turn at a time", "skipping the line", "batching quick Messages" and "a resent Message" groups). The real-Model evals for batching and interrupting are in eval/turn/queue-cases.ts.

What was left out, and what comes next

Steering, a batch cap, a FIFO line shared by other sources, and provenance in the Model's input are left out. Later steps add the wake sources (Routines, Slack, helpers) that need them.
