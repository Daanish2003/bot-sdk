# Durable cron and durable sleep for the runner

Research for [#536](https://github.com/Daanish2003/ai-chat/issues/536) (child of the map [#415](https://github.com/Daanish2003/ai-chat/issues/415)). Researched on 2026-10-18. It builds on [#408](https://github.com/Daanish2003/ai-chat/issues/408), whose file covers each engine's internals ([durable-engine-internals.md](https://github.com/Daanish2003/ai-chat/blob/research/durable-engine-internals/docs/research/always-on-agent/durable-engine-internals.md)).

**Question.** How should the durable runner grow **durable cron** and **durable sleep** for Routines and Check-ins? For each engine: the recurrence format and how the next fire time is computed, time zones and DST, missed fires, per-user schedules, durable sleep, and how a fire is fenced. It ends with options, not a decision.

**Facts only. The owner decides.** A new dependency and a schema change are both owner decisions (AGENTS.md §6).

## Our runner today

- **`schedule(name, { everyMs })`** is declared on `JobRunner` ([jobs/runner.ts#L38-L39](../../packages/bot-sdk/plugins/runner/server/jobs/runner.ts)). The in-process runner throws "Recurring jobs are not supported yet" ([jobs/in-process.ts#L134-L136](../../packages/bot-sdk/plugins/runner/server/jobs/in-process.ts)), and a test pins that ([job-runner.integration.test.ts#L776](../../packages/bot-sdk/test/plugins/runner/server/job-runner.integration.test.ts)). Nothing else calls it.
- **ADR 0011** plans interval-only recurrence: "`schedule(name, { everyMs })`, with no cron. Each slot is enqueued under the key `name:<slot>`". It defers durable sleep and cron to step 4 as new history kinds ([0011#L41](../adr/0011-background-jobs-through-durable-runner.md), [#L70](../adr/0011-background-jobs-through-durable-runner.md)).
- **The history kinds** are `activity | now | random | patched` ([workflow/engine.ts#L9](../../packages/bot-sdk/plugins/runner/server/workflow/engine.ts)).
- **The schema** is `chat.job` plus `chat.job_event`. The idempotency key is unique among `queued` and `running` jobs only. The claim index is on `(status, run_after)` ([db/job.ts](../../packages/bot-sdk/plugins/runner/server/db/job.ts)). There is no schedule table.
- **`enqueueJob` takes a `Database | Transaction`** ([jobs/store.ts#L30-L35](../../packages/bot-sdk/plugins/runner/server/jobs/store.ts)). A job's context is a `WorkflowContext<Transaction>` ([jobs/define.ts#L25-L28](../../packages/bot-sdk/plugins/runner/server/jobs/define.ts)). So a `(tx) =>` activity can enqueue the next job in the same transaction as its Checkpoint.
- **Step 5's Computer sleep (#508) is already a self-chaining job.** Each check enqueues one follow-up with `runAfter`, because "the runner can't reschedule a pending job, and a duplicate idempotency key is a no-op".

## Per engine

### DBOS Transact TS

- **Format:** cron with 5 or 6 fields (seconds optional) and nicknames such as `@daily`. The parser is DBOS's own code, "based on code from node-cron 4.6.0" (ISC) ([src/scheduler/crontab.ts#L1-L30](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/crontab.ts#L1-L30)). The package has no cron dependency ([package.json#L106-L112](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/package.json#L106-L112)). The file is 639 lines.
- **Next fire:** `TimeMatcher.nextWakeupTime` walks the calendar one local day at a time, for at most 100 years. On each matching day it tries the hour, minute and second lists ([crontab.ts#L566-L585](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/crontab.ts#L566-L585)).
- **Time zone and DST:** a schedule has an optional `cronTimezone` (IANA), checked with `Intl.DateTimeFormat`. Without one it uses the process's local zone ([crontab.ts#L377-L385](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/crontab.ts#L377-L385), [#L536-L541](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/crontab.ts#L536-L541)). Wall-clock time comes from `Intl` `formatToParts`. For each day it binary-searches the offset change. The comment reads: "A local time repeated by a fall-back exists under both offsets; one skipped by a spring-forward under neither" ([crontab.ts#L587-L639](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/crontab.ts#L587-L639)).
- **Missed fires:** `automaticBackfill` defaults to `false`. A loop starts from "now", so by default the slots missed while down are skipped. With backfill on, it enqueues every slot from `last_fired_at` to now, each under its slot ID ([scheduler.ts#L174-L195](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/scheduler.ts#L174-L195), [#L370-L404](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/scheduler.ts#L370-L404)).
- **Per-user schedules:** `DBOS.createSchedule({ scheduleName, workflowFn, schedule, context, options })` writes a row to `workflow_schedules` at runtime ([src/dbos.ts#L2306-L2330](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/dbos.ts#L2306-L2330)). Every process polls that table, every 30 s by default, and keeps **one in-memory loop per active schedule** ([scheduler.ts#L96-L99](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/scheduler.ts#L96-L99), [#L160-L215](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/scheduler.ts#L160-L215)). So with N user schedules, each process runs N sleeping loops.
- **Fencing:** each loop sleeps until the next fire plus jitter, then enqueues under the workflow ID `sched-<name>-<ISO time>`. A pre-check is "for performance only, not needed for correctness"; the workflow ID's uniqueness is the fence ([scheduler.ts#L227-L310](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/scheduler/scheduler.ts#L227-L310)).
- **Durable sleep:** `DBOS.sleepms` records the wake time as a step, then waits with an in-process `sleepms` loop ([dbos.ts#L1192-L1210](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/dbos.ts#L1192-L1210), [system_database.ts#L2886-L2894](https://github.com/dbos-inc/dbos-transact-ts/blob/8643d389db64351f2acfce45e96e9747b4be8465/src/system_database.ts#L2886-L2894)). A recovered workflow sleeps only for what is left, but the sleeping workflow stays in memory.

### pg-boss

- **Format:** cron, through **cron-parser**, or an RFC 5545 **RRULE**, through **rrule-temporal**. Both are dependencies ([package.json#L14-L19](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/package.json#L14-L19)). The docs say rules "cover the schedules cron cannot express: the last Friday of the month, every second Monday, a schedule that stops on a date or after a number of runs". Their example is `'RRULE:FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=MO,TU,WE,TH,FR'` with a `DTSTART;TZID=…` ([docs/api/scheduling.md#L31-L54](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L31-L54)).
- **Next fire:** there is no stored next fire time. Every pass reads **every schedule row** and asks the parser for the occurrences in the last 60 seconds ([src/timekeeper.ts#L524-L565](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/src/timekeeper.ts#L524-L565), [#L678-L694](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/src/timekeeper.ts#L678-L694)). Passes run every 30 s, and a schedule sends at most one job a minute ([scheduling.md#L3](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L3)).
- **Time zone and DST:** the `tz` option is checked when `schedule()` is called; UTC is the default. An RRULE `FREQ=DAILY;BYHOUR=9` with `tz` "runs at nine in Chicago, across daylight saving transitions". `HOURLY`/`MINUTELY` intervals count elapsed time, so their local time moves with the offset ([scheduling.md#L54-L60](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L54-L60), [#L132-L137](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L132-L137)).
- **Missed fires:** `missed: 'skip'` is the default ("what every earlier release did"). `missed: 'once'` sends **one** job for the most recent missed slot. The gap runs from the last pass of any instance, and never reaches back past the row's `created_on`. Their reasoning: "a job per missed occurrence would be twelve identical jobs after a twelve-hour outage" ([scheduling.md#L89-L113](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L89-L113), [timekeeper.ts#L696-L723](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/src/timekeeper.ts#L696-L723)).
- **Per-user schedules:** schedules are rows keyed by `(name, key)`; "`key`: an optional unique key if more than schedule is needed for this queue". `getSchedules` pages up to 100,000 ([scheduling.md#L139-L141](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L139-L141), [#L168-L184](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L168-L184)). The cost of a pass grows with the row count, because every pass evaluates every row.
- **Fencing:** one instance wins each pass with `UPDATE … SET cron_on = now() WHERE now() - cron_on >= interval` ([src/plans.ts#L1634](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/src/plans.ts#L1634)). Each job is then deduplicated by a singleton key plus a minute slot. Instance clocks are corrected against the database clock every 10 minutes ([scheduling.md#L19](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss/blob/d4400859eed280b58c346f5289a0312913d558bd/docs/api/scheduling.md#L19)).
- **Durable sleep:** none. pg-boss is a queue; delays are `startAfter` on send (not re-read here).

### graphile-worker

- **Format:** a crontab, with 5 fields **in UTC** only ([website/docs/cron.md#L40-L52](https://github.com/graphile/worker/blob/4cda192c5df254392a1dff350e5d73f7d2c18a85/website/docs/cron.md#L40-L52)). The parser is its own code ([src/cronMatcher.ts](https://github.com/graphile/worker/blob/4cda192c5df254392a1dff350e5d73f7d2c18a85/src/cronMatcher.ts)).
- **Time zone and DST:** none, because everything is UTC. Local "8:00" would move by an hour at each DST change.
- **Missed fires:** skipped by default. `?fill=2d` backfills every slot in that window, and "you cannot say 'backfill at most one job'". Backfill applies only to tasks it already knew ([cron.md#L81-L115](https://github.com/graphile/worker/blob/4cda192c5df254392a1dff350e5d73f7d2c18a85/website/docs/cron.md#L81-L115), [#L187-L199](https://github.com/graphile/worker/blob/4cda192c5df254392a1dff350e5d73f7d2c18a85/website/docs/cron.md#L187-L199)).
- **Per-user schedules:** no. The crontab is a fixed list given at startup, and an identifier is needed per line.
- **Fencing:** a CTE moves `_private_known_crontabs.last_execution` forward only if the new value is newer, and calls `add_job` only for the rows it won ([src/cron.ts#L117-L188](https://github.com/graphile/worker/blob/4cda192c5df254392a1dff350e5d73f7d2c18a85/src/cron.ts#L117-L188), via #408).
- **Durable sleep:** none (`run_at` on a job only).

### Workflow SDK (`@workflow/world-postgres`)

- **Cron:** none. The cookbook builds schedules from `sleep()` in a loop, raced against a cancel hook. It warns: "Scheduled workflows are still pinned to the deployment that started them" ([docs/content/docs/v5/cookbook/common-patterns/scheduling.mdx#L1-L20](https://github.com/vercel/workflow/blob/eb9acbc8ba0041e2cbcd06049c29e56568b7ece3/docs/content/docs/v5/cookbook/common-patterns/scheduling.mdx#L1-L20)).
- **Durable sleep:** `sleep(duration | Date | ms)` is "a built-in runtime function that uses timer events in the event log" ([packages/core/src/sleep.ts#L1-L50](https://github.com/vercel/workflow/blob/eb9acbc8ba0041e2cbcd06049c29e56568b7ece3/packages/core/src/sleep.ts#L1-L50)). It records `wait_created { resumeAt }` and ends the invocation. On a later replay, any wait whose `resumeAt` has passed gets a `wait_completed` event ([packages/core/src/runtime.ts#L3215-L3235](https://github.com/vercel/workflow/blob/eb9acbc8ba0041e2cbcd06049c29e56568b7ece3/packages/core/src/runtime.ts#L3215-L3235)).
- **The wake-up:** the handler returns `{ timeoutSeconds }`, and the Postgres World re-enqueues the same message as a new graphile job with `delaySeconds` ([packages/world-postgres/src/queue.ts#L1015-L1034](https://github.com/vercel/workflow/blob/eb9acbc8ba0041e2cbcd06049c29e56568b7ece3/packages/world-postgres/src/queue.ts#L1015-L1034)). A long sleep is "chained up to 23h" per redelivery. `run.wakeUp()` can complete a wait early ([runtime.ts#L4060-L4072](https://github.com/vercel/workflow/blob/eb9acbc8ba0041e2cbcd06049c29e56568b7ece3/packages/core/src/runtime.ts#L4060-L4072)).

### Hatchet

- **Format:** cron with 5 or 6 fields, through `go-co-op/gocron/v2` ([internal/services/ticker/cron.go#L94-L131](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/4cc02dd69f31d0cfb83fb3ecd32e88f058678e88/internal/services/ticker/cron.go#L94-L131)).
- **Time zone and DST:** "Cron schedules are UTC" ([frontend/docs/content/docs/v1/cron-runs.mdx#L211](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/4cc02dd69f31d0cfb83fb3ecd32e88f058678e88/frontend/docs/content/docs/v1/cron-runs.mdx#L211)). There is no timezone field in the ticker code.
- **Missed fires:** "Hatchet will **not** automatically run the missed instances" ([cron-runs.mdx#L215](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/4cc02dd69f31d0cfb83fb3ecd32e88f058678e88/frontend/docs/content/docs/v1/cron-runs.mdx#L215)).
- **Per-user schedules:** yes. "You can create dynamic cron triggers programmatically via the API … you can have different expressions for different customers." Each trigger returns an ID to store ([cron-runs.mdx#L103-L134](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/4cc02dd69f31d0cfb83fb3ecd32e88f058678e88/frontend/docs/content/docs/v1/cron-runs.mdx#L103-L134)).
- **Fencing:** a ticker claims the crons that have no live ticker with `SKIP LOCKED`, then runs them in an in-memory gocron scheduler in singleton mode ([ticker.sql#L96-L133](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/c205f596ec45936e11044f48e8ac61cc4cdc3f6d/pkg/repository/sqlcv1/ticker.sql#L96-L133) via #408, [cron.go#L18](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/4cc02dd69f31d0cfb83fb3ecd32e88f058678e88/internal/services/ticker/cron.go#L18)).
- **Durable sleep:** a durable task's `sleepFor(duration)` "pauses task execution. While the task is sleeping, no resources are consumed, and the task can also be evicted in order to free the worker slot" ([frontend/docs/content/docs/v1/durable-sleep.mdx](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/4cc02dd69f31d0cfb83fb3ecd32e88f058678e88/frontend/docs/content/docs/v1/durable-sleep.mdx), [sdks/typescript/src/v1/client/worker/context.ts#L1047](https://github.com/hatchet-dev/hatchet/blob/4cc02dd69f31d0cfb83fb3ecd32e88f058678e88/sdks/typescript/src/v1/client/worker/context.ts#L1047)).

### Inngest

- **Format:** 5-field cron plus descriptors, through `robfig/cron/v3` ([pkg/execution/cron/cron.go#L18-L27](https://github.com/inngest/inngest/blob/cd80437eda4559ea571d0b16147c1cc0b1c53a85/pkg/execution/cron/cron.go#L18-L27)).
- **Time zone:** robfig's `Parse` accepts a `TZ=<zone>` or `CRON_TZ=<zone>` prefix ([robfig/cron parser.go#L95](https://github.com/robfig/cron/blob/v3.0.1/parser.go#L95)). Its comments note that DST regimes where midnight doesn't exist cause issues ([spec.go#L114-L123](https://github.com/robfig/cron/blob/v3.0.1/spec.go#L114-L123)).
- **Next fire: a self-chaining queue item.** `ScheduleNext` takes the time embedded in the current item's ULID, computes `Next(expr, from)`, and enqueues the next item under a hashed job ID `{time}:{expr}:{fnID}:{fnVersion}:cron:schedule`. The comment: "We want only one cron loop to exist for a {FnID, FnVersion, CronExpr} combination. This jobID helps achieve that idempotency." It adds deterministic jitter so the item starts slightly early ([pkg/execution/cron/manager.go#L154-L156](https://github.com/inngest/inngest/blob/cd80437eda4559ea571d0b16147c1cc0b1c53a85/pkg/execution/cron/manager.go#L154-L156), [#L314-L345](https://github.com/inngest/inngest/blob/cd80437eda4559ea571d0b16147c1cc0b1c53a85/pkg/execution/cron/manager.go#L314-L345)). Separate health-check jobs re-seed a broken chain ([cron.go#L35-L45](https://github.com/inngest/inngest/blob/cd80437eda4559ea571d0b16147c1cc0b1c53a85/pkg/execution/cron/cron.go#L35-L45)).
- **Per-user schedules:** cron triggers belong to a function definition. Per-user timing goes through an event's `ts` field, the delivery time ([packages/inngest/src/types.ts#L754-L760](https://github.com/inngest/inngest-js/blob/dc1204183c601163b1c4cac3b4330f337d551637/packages/inngest/src/types.ts#L754-L760)), or through `step.sleepUntil`.
- **Durable sleep:** `step.sleep(id, duration)` and `step.sleepUntil(id, date)` return a `Sleep` opcode, and the server resumes the run later. "Durations of less than 1 second round up to `1s`, the minimum resolution of a durable wait" ([packages/inngest/src/components/InngestStepTools.ts#L905-L990](https://github.com/inngest/inngest-js/blob/dc1204183c601163b1c4cac3b4330f337d551637/packages/inngest/src/components/InngestStepTools.ts#L905-L990)).

## Comparison

|                 | Format                                       | Time zone         | Missed fires                   | Per-user rows                                                | Fence                                   | Durable sleep                                |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| DBOS            | cron (own parser, from node-cron)            | IANA per schedule | skip (default) or backfill all | yes, `workflow_schedules`; one loop per schedule per process | workflow ID `sched-<name>-<time>`       | wake time recorded, in-memory wait           |
| pg-boss         | cron (cron-parser) or RRULE (rrule-temporal) | `tz` per schedule | `skip` (default) or `once`     | yes, `(name, key)` rows; every pass scans all                | `cron_on` claim + singleton minute slot | none                                         |
| graphile-worker | cron (own parser)                            | UTC only          | skip or `fill=` all            | no (fixed crontab)                                           | `last_execution` CTE                    | none                                         |
| Workflow SDK    | none (sleep loop)                            | n/a               | n/a                            | n/a                                                          | n/a                                     | `wait_created`, invocation ends, delayed job |
| Hatchet         | cron (gocron)                                | UTC only          | skip                           | yes, via API                                                 | ticker claim `SKIP LOCKED`              | `sleepFor`, slot freed                       |
| Inngest         | cron (robfig)                                | `TZ=` prefix      | not verified (see below)       | via event `ts` / `sleepUntil`                                | deterministic job ID per slot           | `step.sleep`, server resumes                 |

## What "weekdays 8:00" needs that `everyMs` can't express

The lecture's Routine is `prompt + weekdays 8:00` (slide 11, per #415).

1. **Calendar fields.** "Weekdays" skips Saturday and Sunday. A fixed `everyMs` period can't skip days; 24 h fires every day and 120 h drifts through the week.
2. **A time zone.** "8:00" is the user's wall clock. Every engine with a zone stores it per schedule (DBOS `cronTimezone`, pg-boss `tz`, Inngest `TZ=`). Engines in UTC only (graphile-worker, Hatchet) would fire at 7:00 or 9:00 local for half the year.
3. **DST.** In a DST zone, 8:00 local is a different UTC instant in summer and winter, so the UTC gap between two fires is sometimes 23 h or 25 h. That rules out any fixed interval. (8:00 is outside the usual 01:00–03:00 transition hour, so the gap and overlap cases don't arise for this example. They do for a 02:30 Routine.)
4. **Per-user, data-defined schedules.** Each Routine is a user's row, made from chat and approved, so there can be thousands. ADR 0011's `schedule(name, …)` has one `name` and no payload, which fits a fixed job in code (graphile's crontab model), not per-Routine rows (DBOS `createSchedule`, pg-boss `key`, Hatchet API crons).
5. **A missed-fire policy.** Engines default to **skip** (DBOS, pg-boss, graphile, Hatchet). pg-boss also offers `once`, one job for the latest missed slot. Backfilling every slot is opt-in where it exists (DBOS, graphile `fill`). #535 found always-on agents use reschedule, catch-up once, or skip.

## Options for the smallest runner change (not a decision)

**A. A self-chaining job per Routine (Inngest's pattern, and #508's).**

- The Routine row holds its schedule and time zone. Approval enqueues `routine.fire` with `runAfter = next(schedule, tz, now)` and the slot key `routine:<id>:<slot ISO>`.
- When it runs, a `(tx) =>` activity enqueues the next slot in the same transaction as its Checkpoint (`enqueueJob` takes a `Transaction`), then the job fires the Trigger.
- **The fence** is what exists today: the active-unique idempotency key and the `SKIP LOCKED` claim.
- **Missed fires:** after downtime, the one queued job runs late, once, and the chain picks up at the next future slot. That is pg-boss's `once`; skipping instead means comparing the slot with now.
- **Pausing or deleting a Routine:** the fire checks the row's state. Or the next slot's key is stored on the Routine, so `cancel(key)` can find it.
- **Runner change:** none to the schema. `schedule()` could stay a stub or be removed. A next-fire function is still needed: a dependency, or an in-house matcher.
- **Risk:** a chain that breaks (a handler bug before the enqueue, or `onFailed`) stops the Routine. Inngest runs health-check jobs to re-seed chains; #508 re-enqueues from `onFailed`.

**B. A schedule table in the runner (the DBOS, OpenWorkflow, pg-boss and Hatchet pattern).**

- A new `chat.job_schedule` row per `(name, key)` with `schedule`, `timezone`, `payload`, `next_run_at` and `last_fired_at`.
- The poll loop the runner already has also claims due rows with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. In one transaction it enqueues `name` with the key `name:key:<slot>` and moves `next_run_at` forward (OpenWorkflow's shape, via #408).
- `schedule(name, { key, cron, timeZone, payload, missed })` and `unschedule(name, key)` replace `everyMs`.
- An index on `next_run_at` keeps a pass cheap at thousands of rows. pg-boss evaluates every row on every pass; DBOS holds a loop per row.
- **Owner decisions:** a schema change, and a public `JobRunner` API change.

**C. A smaller recurrence format than cron (pairs with A or B).**

- `{ days: [1,2,3,4,5], at: "08:00", timeZone }`, computed with `Intl` and no dependency. It covers "weekdays 8:00" and daily or weekly times.
- It can't say "last Friday" or "every 2nd Monday" (pg-boss's reasons for RRULE). Cron or RRULE could be added later behind the same `next()` seam.
- **Versus a library:** croner has no dependencies, is MIT and 155 kB. cron-parser brings Luxon (4.6 MB unpacked), and its latest 5.10.2 is under two weeks old. An in-house cron is DBOS's 639 lines.

**Durable sleep, separately**

1. **None in step 4.** Check-ins and Routine fires are jobs with `runAfter`. That is what #508 and option A already do, and nothing waits inside a workflow.
2. **A `sleep` history kind**, the pattern of Absurd, OpenWorkflow, the Workflow SDK and Hatchet. `ctx.sleepUntil(date)` records the wake time. The engine then ends the attempt and sets the job back to `queued` with `run_after = wake`, not counting it as a failed attempt. A replay returns at once once the time has passed.
3. **DBOS's in-memory wait** is not advised for this runner. A sleeping job would hold a concurrency slot and a lease heartbeat for the whole wait.

## Unverified

- **Inngest's missed-fire behaviour.** The chain computes the next slot from the item's own timestamp. Whether a late item after downtime walks every missed slot or jumps to the future was not traced. The Inngest server's queue is Redis-backed, not Postgres.
- **DBOS's fall-back hour.** From reading `#firstMatchOnDay`, a local time repeated in a fall-back hour may match under both offsets, which would mean two fires. Not tested.
- **croner and cron-parser DST claims** come from their docs. Their tests were not run.
- **Hatchet's `sleepFor` server-side mechanics** (eviction and resume) were read from docs only.
- **pg-boss `startAfter`** was not re-read for this file.
